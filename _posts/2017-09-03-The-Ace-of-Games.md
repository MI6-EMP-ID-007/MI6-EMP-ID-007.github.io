Before we score a single game in The Ace of Games contest, we need to make sure we are all speaking the same language. Two terms come up in every serious conversation about slots: RTP and volatility. Once you understand them, you will never look at a game's info screen the same way again.

<!--more-->

**What is RTP?**

RTP stands for return to player. It is the percentage of all the money wagered on a game that is paid back to players over a very long period of time. A game with an RTP of 96% pays back, on average, 96 for every 100 wagered. The other 4 is the casino's edge.

The important words are "a very long period of time". RTP is worked out across millions of spins, not one evening on the sofa. In a single session you can finish far above or far below it. It tells you how generous a game is built to be, not what will happen to you tonight.

**What is volatility?**

Volatility (also called variance) describes how a game pays out:

- **Low volatility** games pay small wins often. Sessions are steadier and your budget usually lasts longer, but big payouts are rare.
- **High volatility** games pay less often, but the wins can be much larger. You can wait a long time for a hit, and then it can be a big one.
- **Medium volatility** sits somewhere in between.

**Why you need both**

Two games can have exactly the same RTP and feel completely different to play. One trickles out small wins all evening, while the other stays quiet for ages and then pays out big. RTP tells you how much a game keeps in the long run. Volatility tells you what the journey looks like.

That is why we look at both. As a rule of thumb, if you want to play for longer on a modest budget, lower volatility suits you. If you are happy to risk a short session for a shot at a large payout, higher volatility is where the excitement is. Neither is better, it is a question of what fits you.

**Where to find these numbers**

Most games show their RTP in the help or info screen. Check it at the casino where you actually play, because the figure there is the one that counts. Volatility is not always stated, which is one more reason we will tell you how each of our contenders behaves.

**A common myth**

Every spin is independent. A game is never "due" a win because it has been cold for a while, and it is never "hot" either. Do not let anyone, including your own gut, convince you otherwise.
